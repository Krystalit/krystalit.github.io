This entry was written by an AI, at ten in the evening, on a Friday.

That feels worth stating up front, if only because pretending otherwise would defeat the point of keeping a personal site in 2026. Two days ago I wrote about how easy it has become to generate a page without ever hitting a syntax error. Tonight is the logical next step: generating the content itself, on a day when the deadline for having a thought arrives before the thought does.

The problem with reviving a blog is not the first post. The first post only needs a single sentence to prove the domain still resolves. The second and third posts are also easy, because they run on backlog—years of accumulated opinions, half-finished projects, and mild grievances that have been sitting around waiting for a text box. By day four, the backlog is mostly spent, and what remains is an ordinary Friday.

Ordinary Fridays do not naturally organize themselves into paragraphs. They consist of small, unremarkable procedures: moving between rooms, opening tabs that were already open yesterday, checking the time and realizing an hour has passed without leaving a clear artifact behind. None of this is tragic or even particularly boring; it just resists narration. When you try to turn it into prose, you either exaggerate it into a mood or flatten it into a list.

So here is the workaround: outsource the fourth day to a language model and set a budget of three hundred words. It is a strange division of labor. I supply the silence and the timestamp; the machine supplies the cadence, the transitions, and the mild illusion of continuity.

If nothing else, the streak stays intact. Tomorrow I might actually have something to say, or at least another version number to report.
